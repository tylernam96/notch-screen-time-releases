Privacy

Privacy Policy

Effective 8 October 2026

Your screen time stays on your Mac. There is no account and no analytics, and we have no server that receives your activity. The app only goes online to check your license, look for updates and fetch site icons.

What the app records

Every few seconds NotchUsage notes what's in front of you, so it can show where your time went. It records:

  • The app in front, and how long since you last touched the keyboard or mouse (so time away isn't counted).
  • The front tab in your browser (Safari, Chrome, Arc, Brave or Edge), only after you allow it in macOS's Automation prompt. It reads the address and title of the active tab in the front window. It keeps the site (for example youtube.com) and the page's title, never the full address, never what's on the page, and never your other tabs.
  • What's playing: the title and artist or channel that macOS shows as Now Playing, and which app is playing it. This is how it tells watching from just having a tab open.
  • When you unlock your Mac, and which nudges it showed you.
  • Your settings: categories, goals, keyword rules and preferences.

Private windows: incognito windows in Chrome, Arc, Brave and Edge are skipped; nothing about them is recorded. Safari doesn't tell other apps whether a window is private, so Safari private windows are recorded like any other window. Use Pause Tracking in the menu bar if you'd rather they weren't.

It never records keystrokes, screenshots, screen contents, files, or the contents of web pages.

Where it's kept

All of it is stored on your Mac, in ~/Library/Application Support/NotchUsage/ (your activity in tracking.sqlite, and cached site icons), plus the app's preferences. Detailed samples are kept for 7 days and per-minute summaries for a year. None of it is uploaded, synced or shared, and we can't see it.

What leaves your Mac

Only these three things, and none of them include your activity:

  1. License checks, with Dodo Payments. When you activate a license, the app sends Dodo your license key and your Mac's name (the one in System Settings › General › About). About once a week it sends the key again to confirm it's still valid, and deactivating sends it once more. If you're offline, the app keeps working with the license it has.
  2. Update checks. The app checks for new versions using Sparkle, reading an update feed hosted on GitHub Pages and downloading updates from GitHub. Those requests carry the app's name and version, nothing about how you use it.
  3. Site icons. For a site without a built-in icon, the app downloads that site's own icon (such as example.com/favicon.ico) directly from the site, never through a third-party icon service. The site sees a request from your Mac, as it did when you visited it.

Like any request on the internet, these reveal your IP address to the server that answers them. There's no usage analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking of any kind in the app.

Buying a license

Purchases are handled by Dodo Payments, which is the merchant of record: it takes the payment, handles tax and sends the receipt. Your card and billing details go to Dodo, not to us, and are covered by Dodo's own privacy policy. From Dodo we see your order: your name, email address, country and license key. We use them only to send your license, answer you and handle refunds.

Emailing us

If you write to support, we keep your email to reply and to help if the same problem comes back. We don't add you to any mailing list.

This website

This site has no analytics or cookies. It loads its font from Google Fonts, which sees your IP address like any server would.

Delete your data

  1. If you have a license and are leaving this Mac for good, first deactivate it (below) so it can be used elsewhere.
  2. Quit NotchUsage: menu bar icon › Quit NotchUsage.
  3. Turn off Launch at Login in the app's Settings, or remove NotchUsage in System Settings › General › Login Items.
  4. In Finder, choose Go › Go to Folder…, enter ~/Library/Application Support/NotchUsage and move that folder to the Trash. That's all of your recorded activity.
  5. To remove preferences too, run defaults delete com.tylernam.NotchUsage in Terminal. The license key sits in your login Keychain as com.tylernam.NotchUsage.license; delete it in Keychain Access if you like.
  6. Move NotchUsage from Applications to the Trash, and empty the Trash.

Browser access can be revoked any time in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Automation.

Deactivate a license

Open the menu bar icon › Settings › License › Deactivate on This Mac. This frees that activation with Dodo so you can use the key on another Mac. It needs an internet connection. If the Mac is lost or already wiped, email us and we'll free it for you.

Children

NotchUsage isn't directed at children, and we don't knowingly collect anything from them. Since activity never leaves the Mac, there's nothing about a child's use for us to hold.
